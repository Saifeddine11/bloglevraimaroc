  <p>According to Yakeey's September 2026 estimates, apartments trade on average at <strong>5,771 MAD/sqm in M'Hamid</strong>, <strong>10,055 MAD/sqm in Daoudiat</strong> and <strong>11,029 MAD/sqm in Semlalia</strong> — three neighborhoods often grouped together as "affordable", though the budgets involved differ sharply.</p>

Guéliz, Hivernage and the Palmeraie draw most of the editorial attention on Marrakech real estate, with prices starting at 14,000 MAD/sqm. For a tighter budget, three neighborhoods come up repeatedly: M'Hamid, Daoudiat and Semlalia. The 2026 figures show they are not interchangeable: the price-per-sqm gap between the cheapest and the most expensive of the three exceeds 90%.

## Three neighborhoods, three very different entry budgets

The figures below come from Yakeey's price estimator, continuously updated from transactions and listings observed in Marrakech, accessed on 20 September 2026.

| Neighborhood | Average price (apartment) | Observed range | Average price (villa) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M'Hamid | 5,771 MAD/sqm | 4,021 – 7,239 MAD/sqm | 6,365 MAD/sqm |
| Daoudiat | 10,055 MAD/sqm | 6,179 – 12,731 MAD/sqm | 8,232 MAD/sqm |
| Semlalia | 11,029 MAD/sqm | 7,597 – 14,243 MAD/sqm | 10,058 MAD/sqm |

RICS-certified valuation firm ReaConsult, in its own 2026 reference table, places Semlalia in a similar range (10,000 to 14,000 MAD/sqm) and classes comparable peripheral sectors — Route de Casablanca (6,500 to 10,000 MAD/sqm) and Route de Fès (6,000 to 9,500 MAD/sqm) — in the same price bracket as M'Hamid. Both sources agree on one point: among Marrakech's well-known residential districts, M'Hamid remains one of the cheapest for buying an apartment.

## M'Hamid: the lowest entry budget

At an average under 6,000 MAD/sqm, M'Hamid makes a studio or small one-bedroom (45-50 sqm) achievable for a budget of roughly 230,000 to 360,000 MAD depending on condition. The neighborhood, close to the airport and to established residential areas, mainly serves local demand and long-term tenants rather than tourists.

That is also the main caution flag: the price spread is wide (4,021 to 7,239 MAD/sqm per Yakeey), reflecting very uneven building stock — older walk-up blocks, inconsistent finish quality from one building to the next, and co-ownerships that are not always well run. A tight budget in M'Hamid means checking construction quality and the state of the co-ownership at least as closely as the asking price.

## Daoudiat: a residential district that has caught up

Daoudiat now averages close to 10,055 MAD/sqm, a level comparable to where Semlalia stood two or three years ago. Closer to the city centre than M'Hamid, it benefits from a more uniform residential fabric and steady local demand, driven notably by a student population and middle-class Moroccan families.

The Yakeey range (6,179 to 12,731 MAD/sqm) still shows a wide gap between older buildings and newer developments. For a buyer, this means the same budget can buy very different properties depending on the exact street — something neither the neighborhood's name nor its average price can settle on its own.

## Semlalia: the most expensive of the three, but still below Guéliz

Semlalia remains the priciest of the three neighborhoods studied here, averaging 11,029 MAD/sqm for an apartment — a level ReaConsult confirms (10,000-14,000 MAD/sqm). Its more central location and better connectivity explain the premium over Daoudiat and M'Hamid.

Semlalia is still markedly cheaper than Guéliz (14,000 to 22,000 MAD/sqm per ReaConsult) or Hivernage (18,000 to 26,000 MAD/sqm), making it a middle ground for a buyer who wants to stay close to the centre without paying the entry price of the premium districts.

## What rental yield can you actually expect?

ReaConsult puts the long-term rental yield at 5.5% for Semlalia, and between 7.0% and 7.2% for peripheral sectors comparable to Daoudiat and M'Hamid (Route de Casablanca, Route de Fès) — against 4.0% to 5.2% in premium districts such as the Palmeraie or Guéliz. These are gross figures, before charges, vacancy and taxation.

> "Between the advertised yield and the yield you actually collect, there is vacancy, service charges, local tax and the low season. The gap is often more than two points." — ReaConsult, RICS methodology applied to the Moroccan rental market

In practice, a 7% gross yield advertised on a property in Daoudiat or M'Hamid most often becomes, once co-ownership charges, the local services tax and rental vacancy are deducted, a net yield closer to 4.5-5.5%. ## What to know before buying

Three cautions apply regardless of which of the three neighborhoods you choose:

- **Land title status.** Some of the older building stock in these areas may still be held under moulkiya (unregistered customary title), which complicates bank financing and justifies a price discount. Our [guide to land titles and moulkiya in Marrakech](/en/articles/land-title-moulkiya-marrakech) covers the checks to run before making an offer.
- **Resale liquidity.** These neighborhoods serve local, non-tourist demand: resale can take longer than in a district such as Guéliz or Hivernage, where the buyer pool is broader (residents, the Moroccan diaspora, foreigners).
- **Variable build quality.** The gap between the low and high end of each range — sometimes more than double — shows there is no single "neighborhood price": the building's condition, floor, co-ownership and exact street matter as much as the district's name.

## Key takeaways

1. **M'Hamid** has the lowest entry budget (around 5,800 MAD/sqm), but with uneven building stock that needs checking block by block.
2. **Daoudiat** (around 10,000 MAD/sqm) offers a residential middle ground, with a marked price gap between older and newer buildings.
3. **Semlalia** (around 11,000 MAD/sqm) remains the most expensive of the three, but still 20 to 50% cheaper than Guéliz or Hivernage.
4. The advertised gross yield (7% on the periphery) needs adjusting for charges, vacancy and taxation to reach a realistic net yield (4.5 to 5.5%).
5. Before any purchase, having an independent professional verify the land title status remains the priority, whichever neighborhood you choose.

These three neighborhoods are not suited to a high-end tourist rental project: they follow a residential, local logic, with more modest yields but a noticeably lower entry ticket than the rest of the Marrakech market.
