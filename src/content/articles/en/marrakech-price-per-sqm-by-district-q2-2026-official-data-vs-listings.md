Searching "Marrakech price per m²" returns spectacular ranges, from 6,000 to 28,000 dirhams depending on the site. The catch: no official statistic publishes a price per m² by district. The index of Bank Al-Maghrib and the land registry (ANCFCC) measures changes by city and by property type. Here is how to read both sources side by side without mixing them up.

## What the official index says for the second quarter of 2026

![Avenue Mohammed VI in Marrakech](/images/articles/gueliz-hivernage-palmeraie-investissement.webp)

According to Hespress, which relays the note from Bank Al-Maghrib and the ANCFCC published on 28 August 2026, the national property asset price index rose **0.7%** over the quarter and over one year. Residential prices are up 1% year on year, driven by apartments (+1.1%), while houses (-0.7%) and villas (-0.3%) fell.

In Marrakech, prices gained **0.5%** over the quarter. That is modest: similar to Casablanca (+0.5%) and below Tangier (+2.3%), Rabat (+1.9%) or Fez (+1.7%).

> An asking price is not a sale price: the official index is based on registered transactions, not on sellers' requests.

## Volumes rebound, prices do not surge

Bladi.net, drawing on the same source, reports that sales in Marrakech had fallen 51.5% in the first quarter of 2026 before rebounding **18.3%** in the second, against +11% nationally. According to the article, volume remains around 43% below its end-2025 level.

- Apartments: transactions +13.1%, prices +0.7%.
- Villas: transactions +13.6%, prices -1.3%.
- Houses: transactions +36.5%, prices +2.2%.
- Urban land: transactions +37.8%, prices -0.1%.

The cautious reading: activity is returning, but villa sellers are not getting increases. Bladi's and Hespress's figures use different comparison bases (previous quarter or previous year) and cannot simply be added together.

## District ranges: handle with care

![Modern buildings in Gueliz, Marrakech](/images/articles/marrakech-accessible-investisseurs.webp)

Two agency websites publish district grids. Neither is an official statistic, and they do not agree. L'Agence Immobilière (Morgan & James, June 2026) gives, for new and older apartments:

- Hivernage: 16,000 to 28,000 MAD/m².
- Gueliz: 14,000 to 22,000 MAD/m².
- Victor Hugo / Annakhil: 13,000 to 20,000 MAD/m².
- Agdal: 10,000 to 15,000 MAD/m².
- Targa: 9,000 to 14,000 MAD/m².
- Massira / M'hamid: 6,000 to 9,000 MAD/m².
- Sidi Youssef Ben Ali: 5,500 to 8,500 MAD/m².

Masaken.ma, which cites neither source nor date, gives for example 20,000 to 25,000 MAD/m² in Hivernage, 14,000 to 18,000 in Gueliz and 10,000 to 12,500 in Targa. The gap on Targa (9,000-14,000 versus 10,000-12,500) shows the problem: these are orders of magnitude, not enforceable references. Morgan & James itself says the values should be "validated against the agency's internal records".

## How to use these figures without overpaying

Ranges help place a property, not value it. Between the bottom and top of a range the gap can exceed 50%: floor, condition, new or old, proximity to a main road and land title all matter. The gross yields quoted by agencies (4 to 6% long-term and 6 to 10% short-term at Morgan & James) are commercial estimates: check our calculation of the [net yield after tax in Marrakech](/articles/rendement-net-marrakech-calcul-impot-2026) before relying on them.

In a market where villas are slipping slightly and volumes remain below end-2025, the balance of power is more even than it looks: a well-argued negotiation is possible.

## Checklist before making an offer

- Collect at least **five comparable properties** sold or listed on the same street or in the same complex.
- Ask the agency whether the displayed price is an asking price or a completed sale.
- Compare ranges from two different sources, never just one.
- Check the land title and ownership certificate before any negotiation.
- For a villa, factor in the official trend (-1.3% in Q2 according to Bladi) in your offer.
- Watch for the next Bank Al-Maghrib index release before committing.
