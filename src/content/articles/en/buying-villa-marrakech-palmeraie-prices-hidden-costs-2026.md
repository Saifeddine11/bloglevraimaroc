Looking up the price of a villa in Marrakech quickly gets dizzying: depending on the guide, the Palmeraie runs from 14,000 to 22,000 MAD/m², from 22,000 to 38,000 MAD/m², or up to 76,000 MAD/m² for the most prestigious addresses. These figures are not all wrong; they simply measure different things. Here is how to read them, what the market did over recent months, and which costs never appear in the listing.

## Three ranges, three different realities

Reaconsult's guide (April 2026) values a Palmeraie villa of 600 to 1,000 m² of living space on 2,000 to 5,000 m² of land at 12 to 35 million MAD. Wafir (May 2026) cites 14,000 to 22,000 MAD/m² and, for a 400 to 600 m² villa with a pool, 5 to 12 million MAD. A Knight Frank study relayed by Bladi.net (August 2026) places the top end (Royal Palm, Amelkis, Palmeraie, some medina sectors) at 5,500 to 7,000 €/m², roughly 59,500 to 76,000 MAD/m².

> The 76,000 MAD/m² figure is the top of the ultra-prime range, not an average Palmeraie price.

Reaconsult's own page is also inconsistent: its summary gives 14,000 to 20,000 MAD/m² for the Palmeraie, its table 22,000 to 38,000 MAD/m². Price really depends on three variables that averages blend together: plot size (several thousand m² is common), finish level and exact address.

## A villa market stable on prices, uneven on volumes

Official figures mostly say one thing: villa prices move little, sales move a lot. For Q1 2026, Bladi.net reports, citing Bank Al-Maghrib and ANCFCC, Marrakech villa prices up 0.1% on the quarter while transactions fell 53.2%. In Q2, the ANCFCC index shows Marrakech villa prices down 1.3% and transactions up 13.6% versus the previous quarter.

- Q1 2026: prices +0.1%, transactions -53.2%.
- Q2 2026: prices -1.3%, transactions +13.6%.
- Knight Frank reports a 10 to 15% rise over two years for the prestige segment and about 6% expected in 2026: a firm's forecast, not an observed result.

These series are short and based on few sales: one quarter is not a trend. For a buyer, the prudent reading is that sellers are not forced to cut prices, but buyers can take time to negotiate.

## Costs that are not in the asking price

![Moroccan dirham banknotes, illustrating the extra costs of buying a villa in Marrakech.](/images/articles/acheter-villa-marrakech-prix-palmeraie-couts-caches-2026-3.webp)

On an unserviced Palmeraie plot, Reaconsult mentions hidden costs of 200,000 to 800,000 MAD (water, sanitation, electricity connection). This is the first item to have quoted in writing, by a professional independent of the seller, before signing. The usual acquisition costs come on top: registration duties, notary fees and land registry fees, covered in our other articles and applicable to a villa too.

I found no reliable source putting a figure on a villa's yearly upkeep (pool, garden, guarding): ask the seller for actual invoices from the past two years rather than rely on a generic estimate.

## Yield: the gap between long-term and seasonal

Sources agree on the principle. On long-term rental, a villa earns little: about 3.8% gross per Reaconsult, 3 to 4% per Wafir. On tourist rental, Reaconsult cites 7 to 11% gross and Knight Frank 7 to 10% gross. These are gross yields, before platform commissions, cleaning, staff, taxes and slow periods. As a benchmark, Wafir uses about 40% costs for an Airbnb in Hivernage, a figure for an apartment that does not transfer directly to a villa. To go from gross to net, see our articles on net yield and on Airbnb and the Foreign Exchange Office.

## Checklist before buying a villa

- Ask for an up-to-date land title and compare it with the real plot area: on large plots, discrepancies exist.
- Get written quotes for connections (water, sanitation, electricity) if not in place.
- Check the building permit and the occupancy permit (see our dedicated article).
- Obtain upkeep, staff and water invoices for the past two years.
- Compare at least three like-for-like listings (same plot, same finish) rather than an average price per m².
- Compute net yield on a prudent basis, and do not count on a quick resale: sales volumes remain uneven.

These figures are orders of magnitude from public guides and studies; they do not replace a survey or personalised legal or tax advice.
