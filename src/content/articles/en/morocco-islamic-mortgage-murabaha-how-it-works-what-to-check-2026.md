To finance an apartment or villa in Marrakech, most buyers think of a conventional loan first. Yet murabaha home financing, offered by Morocco's participative banks, now amounts to tens of billions of dirhams. It works differently from an interest-bearing loan, and that changes what you need to compare before signing. Below are the principle, the official market figures and concrete checkpoints. Exact margins and taxes depend on the bank and the date: this article says what can be established, and what cannot.

## The principle: the bank buys, then resells to you with a margin fixed in advance

In a home murabaha the bank does not lend you money. It buys the property, then resells it to you at a higher price than its acquisition cost, which you pay in instalments. The difference is the bank's margin, known and fixed at signing. Finances News Hebdo describes the double transfer: the bank acquires the property and registers it on the land title, then transfers it to the customer.

The same logic exists in other forms. The Moroccan press also mentions Ijara Mountahia Bitamlik (lease with a promise of sale) and Moucharaka moutanakissa (diminishing co-ownership). According to the comparison site Wafir, participative banks' products must be validated by the Higher Council of Ulema, and the legal framework for participative banks sits in Law 103-12 on credit institutions.

> A murabaha is not "an interest-free loan": it is a sale at a marked-up price, paid in instalments. The cost exists; it is simply presented as a margin. The only honest comparison is the total cost.

## A market that has become large

Bank Al-Maghrib figures, relayed by the press, show steady growth in "participative financing for housing, notably in the form of home murabaha (including deferred margins)":

- **End of July 2026**: MAD 32.1 billion, versus MAD 27.4 billion a year earlier, i.e. **+17.1%** (Hespress, 1 September 2026).
- **End of June 2026**: MAD 31.5 billion, versus MAD 27 billion a year earlier, i.e. **+16.7%** (Boursenews, 12 August 2026).
- **2025**: MAD 29.7 billion after MAD 24.9 billion a year earlier, i.e. **+19.3%** (Finances News Hebdo, 10 February 2026).

For comparison, household credit grew 3.3% year on year at end-July 2026 and housing loans 2.6% (Hespress). The scopes are not identical, but the gap in pace suggests participative banks are gaining ground. This figure measures outstanding balances, not a price advantage: it says nothing about what you will pay.

## Margin, instalment, total cost: how to compare

Margins quoted by comparison sites vary widely. On Wafir's pages, published ranges run from about 4% to more than 6% depending on the page and its update date. No single figure is a reference: ask each bank for a written offer showing the margin, term and total cost.

An illustrative example, taken from a Wafir simulation and recalculated: financing MAD 720,000 over 25 years at a fixed 5.00% margin gives an instalment of about MAD 4,210 (total cost MAD 1,263,000), versus about MAD 4,000 for a conventional loan at 4.50% (MAD 1,200,000). The gap is MAD 63,000. These rates are hypothetical, not offers: the point is that half a percentage point can shift the total cost by tens of thousands of dirhams.

To place the market, Boursenews reports an average rate of 4.53% on housing loans in Q2 2026. Keep three comparison rules in mind:

- Always compare the **total cost** over the term, including arrangement fees and insurance, not just the instalment.
- Check whether the margin is **fixed**: according to Wafir it is set at the outset and is not renegotiated like a conventional loan rate.
- Read the **early repayment** clause: on Wafir's pages, conditions vary (a discount on the remaining margin or a fee); there is no single rule.

## Costs and tax: two property transfers to clarify

![Moroccan land documents to verify before the double property transfer of a murabaha](/images/articles/dahir-maroc-immobilier-acheteur.webp)

The delicate point is the double transfer: the bank buys, then resells to you. Tax treatment has changed over time. Article 7 of the 2009 Finance Act provided that registration duties would be levied only once, according to La Vie éco. For social housing, a March 2018 tax authority note provided a VAT exemption on the bank's purchase, a reduced 3% registration duty and a fixed MAD 200 duty on the transfer to the customer (Finances News Hebdo).

I could not verify the exact regime applying in 2026 to a non-social property, such as a villa or a higher-end apartment in Marrakech. The sources consulted are old or contradict each other. Ask the bank and the notary for a **written breakdown**: registration duties, land registry fees, any VAT on the margin, and notary fees for each of the two deeds. There is no shortcut here.

## Checklist before signing

![Signing a property financing contract at a notary in Morocco](/images/articles/charges-copropriete-gestion-locative-marrakech-2.webp)

- Get a written offer with margin, term, down payment, fees, insurance and **total cost**.
- Compare at least one participative bank and one conventional bank, with the same term and down payment.
- Ask for the breakdown of the **two deeds** (purchase by the bank, sale to you) and their costs.
- Read the early repayment and late payment conditions.
- Have the land title checked before the bank buys the property.
- Have the contract reviewed by a notary independent of the bank.
- Check the impact on your budget: a lower instalment does not mean a lower cost.
