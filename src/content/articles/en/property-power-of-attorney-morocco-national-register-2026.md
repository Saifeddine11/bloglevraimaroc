Buying or selling in Marrakech without being on site almost always goes through a power of attorney. It is also one of the most exposed links in the chain: forged mandates, mandates used beyond their purpose, or used long after signing. Since 1 June 2026, Morocco has framed this with a national register. Here is what the sources say, where they disagree, and how to allow for it before you sign.

## What changed on 1 June 2026

According to Hespress, the national register of powers of attorney came into force on 1 June 2026 in all courts of first instance across the Kingdom. It rests on law no. 31.18, which supplements the Code of Obligations and Contracts. Captain.Legal adds decree no. 2.23.101 and ministerial circular no. 205 of 1 April 2026; Le360 mentions a ministerial decision published in the Official Bulletin on 6 October 2025.

The register covers powers of attorney used to sell, buy, donate, mortgage or carry out any other operation on a real property right. Ordinary powers (bank account, civil status) are excluded, according to Captain.Legal.

> According to Hespress, the register should make it possible to check that a power of attorney exists, what it covers, and whether it has been cancelled or amended.

The register does not replace the Land Registry: it publishes the authority given to a third party, not ownership. For the title itself, see our guide to [land title and moulkiya](/articles/titre-foncier-moulkiya-marrakech).

## Form: authentic deed, not a legalised private paper

![Documents and deeds linked to a property power of attorney in Morocco](/images/articles/procuration-immobiliere-maroc-registre-national-acheter-vendre-2026-2.webp)

Le360 reports that powers of attorney must be drawn up in authentic form on pain of nullity, with a notary, an adoul or a lawyer admitted before the Court of Cassation. Captain.Legal specifies that a private-signature power with a legalised signature is not enough to sell. FNH ties the requirement of an authorised professional to the 2017 rules, and adds that the power must state the purpose of the transaction and be limited in time.

This follows the same logic as [law 41.25 on the authentic deed](/articles/loi-41-25-acte-authentique-immobilier-maroc): fewer private papers, more identifiable professionals.

## What if the power of attorney is not registered?

The sources do not say quite the same thing:

- **Bladi.net**: any transaction by proxy requires prior registration of the power of attorney.
- **FNH**: an unregistered power of attorney is considered invalid and cannot support a valid transaction.
- **Captain.Legal**: an unregistered mandate stays valid between principal and agent but cannot be enforced against third parties; the registrar may refuse the formality.

All three converge on the practical result: a transaction on an unregistered mandate risks being blocked. For earlier powers, Captain.Legal says the obligation applies to acts carried out from 1 June 2026. None of these sources gives the registration fees; ask the notary.

## Buying or selling from abroad: limit the powers

![Stamped passport, illustrating a remote purchase or sale by a Moroccan abroad or a foreigner](/images/articles/procuration-immobiliere-maroc-registre-national-acheter-vendre-2026-3.webp)

ReaConsult recommends a mandate covering a single property, identified with its land title references, with a precise list of the acts covered (preliminary agreement, final deed, financing where relevant), a minimum price for a sale or a maximum price for a purchase, and the account receiving the funds named explicitly. It describes the general power of attorney as the best-documented abuse pattern among MRE, and advises a term of 3 to 6 months, renewable: a power with no end date remains usable "years later". LesMRE likewise asks for a clause strictly limiting the authorised acts to the property concerned.

Two further precautions from ReaConsult: no money should move outside the notary's channel, and you should ask for the draft deed 48 to 72 hours before signing. ReaConsult is a firm that also sells valuations; its advice is good practice, not a legal requirement.

## Point to check: the consulate

On the consular route, the sources contradict each other. ReaConsult (12 June 2026) still describes signing before the consul (usual lead time 2 to 3 weeks) or a local notarial power that is apostilled or legalised. Captain.Legal states the opposite: consulates have no longer issued authentic property powers of attorney since 2019, and the deed must be drawn up in Morocco. We cannot settle this: ask the Moroccan notary who will receive the deed which form they accept, before you travel or pay anything.

## Remedies in case of fraud

LesMRE cites a criminal complaint for fraud (article 540 of the Penal Code) as the first remedy, with a limitation period of 5 years from discovery, and says MRE can file a complaint from abroad through Moroccan consulates. FNH recalls that a monitoring commission has existed since 2016 and that the Land Registry offers online tracking of the title. None of the sources consulted gives a figure for spoliation cases.

## Checklist before signing or accepting a power of attorney

- Check that the power is **authentic** (notary, adoul or approved lawyer) and **entered in the register** before any deed.
- Require a **precise description of the property** (title references) and the list of authorised acts.
- Set a **minimum or maximum price** and a **named bank account** for the funds.
- Limit the **term** (3 to 6 months per ReaConsult) and avoid any general power.
- Have the **title** and the ownership certificate checked before any payment, see [prenotation](/articles/prenotation-titre-foncier-proteger-compromis-acompte-marrakech).
- Check the notary on the **Chamber of Notaries register**, not through the intermediary.
- Pay nothing outside the **notary's channel** and ask for the draft deed 48 to 72 hours ahead.
- Confirm the **accepted form** (consulate or local notary) with the Moroccan notary; see also [the purchase procedure for foreigners and MRE](/articles/procedure-achat-immobilier-marrakech-etranger-mre-2026).

*This article is informational and does not replace advice from a notary or lawyer. The implementing rules and how courts and the Land Registry read them are evolving: check the rule that applies to your case.*
