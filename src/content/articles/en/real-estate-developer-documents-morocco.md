Before reserving, ask for developer identity, land title, proof of ownership or building right, permit, approved plans, specifications, contract, payment schedule, progress, delivery date, co-ownership rules and title-division process.</p>

## Why verification is essential

Buying from a developer in Morocco can be attractive: new housing, staged payments, modern residence, shared amenities. But the risk rises as soon as the buyer commits to a property not yet delivered or to an incomplete file.

A brochure is not a legal document. A 3D rendering is not an approved plan. A verbal promise is not a contract. A “secured plot” must be readable in a title, certificate or notarial document.

## Document-by-document checklist

Check the developer’s company identity, land title, ownership or building right, land registry information, building permit, approved plans, project specifications, reservation contract, payment schedule, construction progress, delivery date, co-ownership rules, habitation permit, title subdivision and the role of the notary. If a point remains unclear, slow down and ask for a written explanation.

## Warning signs

<div class="article-warning">
  <span class="article-kicker">Red Flags</span>
  <ul>
    <li>Refusal to share documents before payment.</li>
    <li>Price far below market without verifiable explanation.</li>
    <li>Guaranteed return without a solid contract.</li>
    <li>Permit or plans “in progress” presented as obtained.</li>
    <li>Payment requested to an unclear account.</li>
    <li>Excessive pressure to sign quickly.</li>
  </ul>
