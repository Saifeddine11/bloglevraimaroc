Erfoud is reported as a filming stop, but no exact set or local schedule has been made public.

## Key points

- Erfoud is named in a report published on 14 September 2026.

- No Erfoud filming day has been published.

- Merzouga and Erg Chebbi are not confirmed sets.

- The landscape's exact narrative role is unknown.

## Erfoud as a logistical gateway to a vast territory

Erfoud has long served as an access point for the Tafilalet and areas near Erg Chebbi. Its production value is not limited to dunes: straight roads, palm groves, rocky relief, ksour and dry light widen the visual vocabulary. A town base also helps organise accommodation, supplies and crew movements before reaching more isolated sites.

Le360 names Erfoud among the selected places without giving a site, local date or sequence. The Merzouga image used here establishes regional context; it does not prove that cameras were placed there. That distinction protects both editorial accuracy and the discretion required during an active production.



## What the evidence supports

Netflix's 23 August announcement establishes that Season 3 exists and that Guy Ritchie is returning to direct. Netflix Tudum provides further official context. The Moroccan locations have a different evidence level: Le360 reported on 14 September that filming had begun in Rabat the previous day and that Erfoud was also selected. First-party material supplied to Le Vrai Maroc supports the existence of a vehicle operation in Morocco.

These layers are deliberately kept separate. An official renewal does not automatically confirm every location, and a production-service image does not establish what will appear in the series. Dates, places and relationships are attributed to the source that supports them.

## What remains unconfirmed

No public source reviewed gives a complete Moroccan schedule, precise shooting addresses, scene descriptions or a final vehicle list. Netflix has not publicly confirmed Accrocar as a partner.

Published images are privacy-safe derivatives: metadata was stripped and crops exclude visible plates, people and sensitive site identifiers. Originals remain outside the public website tree.
Erfoud illustrates the challenge of working away from major centres: crews must plan for autonomy, distance, dust, heat and transport continuity.


## How we verified this information

We compared three evidence sets: Netflix announcements, public institutional or media sources, and first-party material supplied by Accrocar. That method explains why “Erfoud is named in a report published on 14 September 2026.” and “No Erfoud filming day has been published.” may carry different confirmation levels. A date published by Netflix is official; a location named by one outlet remains attributed; an original photograph establishes what is inside the frame, not what happened beyond it.

Every image was checked for vehicle identity, context and sensitive details. We excluded files that revealed a hotel, operational site, registration plate or identifiable person. We did not use an unverified social caption or visual guess to manufacture an address. This restraint makes the story less sensational, but more durable and easier to update when new official information appears.

## Why this matters for Morocco

The case also exposes a chain of skills: scouting, line production, unit management, technical crews, accommodation, security, transport and specialised suppliers. An international series does not relocate for one panorama; it assesses whether a territory can repeat a reliable working day.

Rabat and Erfoud test that capacity in different ways. The capital concentrates institutions and services in a varied urban setting; the south-east demands autonomy and control of distance. The professional and economic significance lies in continuity between places, crews and Moroccan businesses, not in an unverifiable promotional claim.
