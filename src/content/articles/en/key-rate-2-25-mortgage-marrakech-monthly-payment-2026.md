On 22 September 2026, Bank Al-Maghrib's Council left its key rate at **2.25%**, the sixth decision in a row with no change. For a buyer in Marrakech the good news is the absence of bad news: no hike in sight. But that figure is not what your bank will charge you, and understanding the gap matters before you sign a loan offer.

## What Bank Al-Maghrib decided, and why

![Bank Al-Maghrib headquarters in Rabat](/images/articles/taux-directeur-2-25-credit-immobilier-marrakech-mensualite-2026.webp)

According to Le Matin, average inflation reached only 0.3% over the first eight months of 2026. The central bank forecasts 0.7% for the full year and 1.5% in 2027. Inflation this low leaves little reason to raise rates, and projected growth (4.4% in 2026, 2.9% in 2027) does not argue for an immediate cut either.

On financing, the central bank projects bank credit to the non-financial sector accelerating: +8.1% in 2026 versus 4.8% in 2025, then +6.1% in 2027. Recovering credit means active banks and mortgage files that are reasonably well received, but no price war is announced.

> The key rate is a cost floor for banks, not the price you pay: between the two sit the bank's margin, insurance and the borrower's profile.

## Why you do not borrow at 2.25%

The Wafir mortgage observatory (24 August 2026), based on Bank Al-Maghrib statistics and the Afdal barometer, gives these orders of magnitude:

- Average market rate: **4.64%** in the first half of 2026, versus 4.59% in the second half of 2025;
- Average lending rate on mortgages: **5.06%** in Q2 2026 (5.13% in Q1), a series that includes developers;
- Range by profile: from **3.75%** (permanent-contract employee, high income, large deposit) to **4.80%** (un-negotiated counter offer), with most offers between 4.10% and 4.50%;
- Dedicated MRE (Moroccans living abroad) grids: **3.55% to 4.30%**.

These series do not cover the same scope and are not directly comparable, as Wafir itself notes. The key point is that monetary policy pass-through is partial. Since June 2024, according to Medi1 News, lending rates had fallen 58 basis points by end-September 2025, against 75 points for the key rate.

## What one point of difference means on 1 million dirhams

![Moroccan 50-dirham banknotes](/images/articles/taux-directeur-2-25-credit-immobilier-marrakech-mensualite-2026-2.webp)

Here is a simulation from our own calculation (constant payments, insurance and fees excluded) for a loan of 1,000,000 MAD:

| Rate | Term | Monthly payment | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.75% | 25 years | 5,141 MAD | 542,394 MAD |
| 4.20% | 20 years | 6,166 MAD | 479,770 MAD |
| 4.60% | 20 years | 6,381 MAD | 531,344 MAD |
| 5.00% | 20 years | 6,600 MAD | 583,894 MAD |
| 5.00% | 25 years | 5,846 MAD | 753,770 MAD |

Two lessons. First, between 4.20% and 5.00% over 20 years, the payment moves by 434 MAD but the total cost by more than **100,000 MAD**. Second, stretching from 20 to 25 years at 5% lowers the payment by 754 MAD at the price of almost 170,000 MAD more interest. Wafir notes a gap of about 175,000 MAD between 3.75% and 4.80% over 25 years: our calculation gives 176,600 MAD.

## Making use of it in Marrakech

![Signing a document linked to a property purchase](/images/articles/taux-directeur-2-25-credit-immobilier-marrakech-mensualite-2026-3.webp)

A stable key rate gives visibility, not a gift. The lever remains your file and your ability to make banks compete. For a rental property, stress-test the loan with a lower rent and two months of vacancy: our [net rental yield analysis](/en/articles/net-rental-yield-marrakech-tax-calculation-2026) explains the method. If you finance from abroad, see our guide to [mortgages for MRE and foreigners](/en/articles/mortgage-marrakech-foreigners-mre-2026), and for more simulations our [September 2026 mortgage update](/en/articles/morocco-mortgage-rates-september-2026).

One caveat: the rates above are published averages and ranges, not offers. No bank guarantees these levels, and the next Council meeting can change the picture.

## Checklist before signing a loan offer

- Ask for **three written offers** with nominal rate, insurance and arrangement fees quantified.
- Require the **amortisation schedule** and check the amount actually borrowed.
- Compare the **total cost** (interest + insurance + fees), not just the monthly payment.
- Get the **early repayment** terms in writing.
- Test your budget with a rate one point higher and rent 20% lower.
- Keep a margin for acquisition costs, which come on top of the price, and do not neglect negotiating the property price itself.
