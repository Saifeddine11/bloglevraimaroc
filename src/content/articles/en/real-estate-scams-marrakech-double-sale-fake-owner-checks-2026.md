Most property scams in Marrakech are not a total forgery: they exploit a link in the chain the buyer cannot control, especially from abroad. Here are the schemes described by the sources consulted, then the three checks that block them. None of these sources gives a reliable statistic on the number of cases: this guide describes mechanisms, not frequency.

## The five most commonly described schemes

![Land documents and deeds linked to a property purchase in Morocco](/images/articles/arnaques-immobilieres-marrakech-double-vente-faux-proprietaire-verifications-2026-2.webp)

BuyPropertyMorocco (July 2026), ReaConsult and Masaken broadly agree on the same list:

- **Double sale**: the same property is promised or sold to several buyers, each paying a deposit.
- **Fake owner or intermediary without a mandate**: a relative, tenant, caretaker or simsar presents a property they cannot sell. Compare the seller's identity with the one on the title and demand a written mandate.
- **Forged or expired power of attorney**: a mandate limited to another act, revoked, or fabricated. According to FNH, networks use fake ID documents and fake powers of attorney, sometimes with inside help.
- **Untitled property presented as titled**: ReaConsult reminds buyers that a moulkia establishes possession, which can be contested, not a definitive title. See our guide to [land title and moulkiya](/articles/titre-foncier-moulkiya-marrakech).
- **The heir or co-owner who sells alone**: BuyPropertyMorocco cites the common case of medina riads held in joint ownership, where a single heir signs.

Common warning signs add to these: artificial urgency ("other buyers decide this week"), a deposit requested before any visit or into a personal account, a copied listing with stolen photos, refusal of a live visit. BuyPropertyMorocco stresses that a video call alone is not enough.

## Check no. 1: a same-day ownership certificate

The ANCFCC ownership certificate lists the owner and the mortgages, seizures, easements and oppositions registered on the title. According to ReaConsult (12 June 2026):

- the fixed fee is **100 DH** per certificate;
- at the land registry counter, the lead time is **2 days**; this is the only route for non-digitised titles;
- online (ANCFCC portal), it is possible only if the title lies in a digitised perimeter, which is not the case everywhere, notably in old medinas; the PDF carries a verification code;
- it reflects the position **on the date of issue**: ReaConsult recommends less than 30 days for a preliminary agreement and a certificate from the day before or the same day for the authentic deed.

Le360 already noted in 2018 that each certificate carries a barcode allowing its authenticity to be checked on the ANCFCC website. A registered opposition blocks any transfer; a mortgage must be discharged before the deed, as detailed in [our article on mortgaged properties](/articles/acheter-bien-hypotheque-marrakech-mainlevee-certificat-propriete). Limit: the certificate says nothing about the real surface area or the condition of the building. And an unregistered title has no certificate to consult: the risk then shifts to the price.

## Check no. 2: a power of attorney entered in the national register

Since **1 June 2026**, according to Le360, Bladi and FNH, any power of attorney relating to a sale or a real right over property must be entered in a national register run by the Ministry of Justice (law 31-18, amending the Code of Obligations and Contracts). Without registration, Le360 describes it as legally void. A revocation only takes effect against third parties once the platform is updated, and notaries, adouls and land registry officials must consult the system before acting (Bladi).

Since 2017, the power of attorney must also be drawn up by an approved lawyer, an adoul or a notary, specify the transaction and have a limited duration (Le360, FNH). The detail is in [our property power of attorney guide](/articles/procuration-immobiliere-maroc-registre-national-acheter-vendre-2026). Do not settle for a copy: have the notary confirm that the mandate is registered, not revoked, and covers both the sale and the collection of the price. A power of attorney, ReaConsult reminds us, does not replace checking the title.

## Check no. 3: authentic deed and traceable payment

![Carved traditional riad door in Marrakech, a symbol of a purchase to be secured](/images/articles/arnaques-immobilieres-marrakech-double-vente-faux-proprietaire-verifications-2026-3.webp)

According to the analysis we already detailed in [our article on law 41.25](/articles/loi-41-25-acte-authentique-immobilier-maroc), the sale, the promise to sell and linked powers of attorney require an authentic deed received by a notary or an adoul. An "agency agreement" without a public officer therefore does not carry the same weight; see also [sale agreement and promise to sell](/articles/compromis-promesse-vente-marrakech-acompte-conditions-suspensives-2026).

For the money, Masaken recommends: no cash, traceable bank transfers, going through a notarial escrow account or a bank, and no payment before a visit. Against double sales, the preliminary contract should be drawn up by the notary and the property protected during the procedure, for instance through [a prenotation](/articles/prenotation-titre-foncier-proteger-compromis-acompte-marrakech).

## If the damage is done

Masaken advises keeping all documents and exchanges, filing a complaint quickly, asking the bank to block the payment if possible and consulting a property lawyer. FNH notes that a watch commission (DGSN, Royal Gendarmerie, Land Registry, Interior) reviews suspicious transactions and that the Land Registry offers online title tracking to spot any change.

## Checklist before any deposit

- Ownership certificate under 30 days old, compared with the seller's ID.
- Agreement of all registered heirs or co-owners.
- Authentic, limited power of attorney, entered in the register, confirmed by the notary.
- Written mandate from any intermediary; ask the agent for their professional card.
- Physical visit of the property, not just a video call.
- No payment before the deed or outside the notarial channel; never cash.
- A notary chosen by you, not imposed by the seller or intermediary.
- Golden rule: if the seller pushes, that is the moment to slow down.
