An apartment in a Guéliz or Hivernage residence is usually negotiated on price, view and pool. Yet it is the condominium (*copropriété*), its rules and its accounts, that decides what you will really pay once you own. This article complements our analysis of [service charges and rental management](/articles/charges-copropriete-gestion-locative-marrakech): here the focus is **what to check before signing**.

## The legal framework: Law 18-00, amended in 2016

Condominiums in built properties are governed by Law No. 18-00, promulgated by Dahir No. 1-02-298 of 3 October 2002 (source: Housing Ministry). It was amended by Law 106-12 (Dahir No. 1-16-49, consolidated text published in Official Bulletin No. 1664 of 3 November 2016). That consolidated text applies today.

![Residence with shared areas in Marrakech](/images/articles/charges-copropriete-gestion-locative-marrakech.webp)

A further reform, often called "Law 30-24", was adopted by the House of Representatives in July 2024 (Bladi.net, 11 July 2024). It provides that one or more owners may call a general meeting with 15 days' notice, that decisions be notified within 8 days, and that conciliation precede any court action. However, ReaConsult wrote on 17 June 2026 that it had been neither promulgated nor published in the Official Bulletin, and our research found **no official confirmation of publication**. Treat these points as forthcoming, not settled.

## The bylaws: the first document to read

Every building must have bylaws (*règlement de copropriété*, Article 8). According to the consolidated text, they set the use of private and common parts, each unit's share, the rules of the general meeting, how the syndic is chosen and how charges are split (Article 9). A technical file (plans, building permit, division record) is attached.

Two points matter to a buyer. First, the seller must give you a copy of the bylaws and plans, and the sale deed must state that you have read them (Article 11). Second, a clause that unduly restricts the use of your unit is void, except as regards the building's purpose. If you plan short-term rentals, read the building's designated use (residential, mixed) first: see [the short-term rental licence](/articles/licence-location-courte-duree-marrakech-loi-80-14).

## Who decides what: the majorities to know

![Signing a legal document](/images/articles/charges-copropriete-gestion-locative-marrakech-2.webp)

Under the consolidated text, routine maintenance and security decisions need a simple majority (Article 20). Three-quarters of votes are required to amend the bylaws, appoint or dismiss the syndic, and vote the budget, the syndic's fees, the major-works reserve or improvement works (Articles 19 and 21). Unanimity is required to build, add floors or alter common parts, and for full demolition (Article 22).

In practice, a minority owner can be bound by a budget or works approved by three-quarters, but nobody can change common parts without you where unanimity applies. Each year the meeting votes a forecast budget and a provision for major works (Article 24); an optional reserve account can cover urgent expenses (Article 37 bis).

## Arrears: the risk you inherit

This is the most sensitive point. Article 42 provides that, on transfer of a unit, **the buyer is jointly liable with the seller** for the seller's debts to the association. ReaConsult notes that this liability arises by law and need not be written into the deed. The association's claims lapse after five years from their approval by the meeting (Article 43), and it can obtain a forced mortgage over the debtor's unit (Articles 40 and 40 bis).

The scale of the problem is acknowledged: AMCOP president Dalila Ennaciri calls unpaid charges the "main problem" of Moroccan condominiums (Bladi.net, 26 June 2024). We found no reliable statistic, so we cite none.

## Documents to demand

According to ReaConsult (a consultancy that sells audits, so cross-check with your notary), ask for at least:

- the **syndic's discharge receipt**, dated within 30 days, showing the unit is up to date;
- the unit's account statement for the last 24 months;
- the minutes of the last five general meetings;
- the bylaws and any amendments;
- current budgets and the state of the works fund;
- the list of disputes, claims and works voted but not carried out.

If the syndic refuses the discharge, the suggested remedy is to settle the debt, for instance by deduction from the sale price. Also specify in the preliminary agreement who pays works voted before the sale but called afterwards: see [the sale agreement](/articles/compromis-promesse-vente-marrakech-acompte-conditions-suspensives-2026).

## Checklist before signing

- Read the bylaws and the building's designated use (short-term rental allowed or not).
- Obtain the syndic's discharge dated within 30 days.
- Review meeting minutes for voted works and disputes.
- Check whether a major-works provision exists.
- Have the deed include a seller warranty for undisclosed association debts.
- Have a notary validate the file; see [the notary's role](/articles/notaire-achat-immobilier-maroc-role-honoraires).
