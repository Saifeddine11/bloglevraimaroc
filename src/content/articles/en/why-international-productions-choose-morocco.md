The Gentlemen shoot highlights an advantage built from landscapes, technicians and a specialised supplier chain.

## Key points

- High visual diversity within one territory.

- Decades of accumulated experience with foreign productions.

- A conditional financial support mechanism administered by the CCM.

- A local network spanning studios and specialised transport.

## An advantage that goes beyond scenery

Morocco's first argument is landscape density: coast, medinas, modern cities, mountains, desert spaces and road infrastructure can be combined within one country. Attractive scenery alone does not move a series, however. Productions also need workable permits, technicians, workshops, equipment, transport, accommodation and partners able to answer late requests.

The Moroccan Cinema Centre describes support of up to 30% of certain eligible local expenditure, subject to conditions. That number is an administered incentive, not an automatic refund. The real competitive edge comes from the combination of cost, experience, variety and service continuity.

Official credits from [Dune Films](https://dunefilms.com/) provide a concrete measure of experience available in Morocco: the Marrakech-based company, with a base in Ouarzazate and active since 1998, lists Napoleon, Game of Thrones, Aladdin, John Wick: Chapter 3 and Heart of Stone among productions it has supported. Those credits prove nothing about The Gentlemen locations; they document the depth of the production network a new series can draw on.



## What the evidence supports

Variety confirmed on 24 August 2026 that The Gentlemen had been renewed for a third season of eight episodes, with Guy Ritchie directing and co-writing with Matthew Read. Netflix Tudum later published an official Season 3 briefing. Moroccan locations sit on a different evidence layer: on 14 September 2026 Le360 reported that filming had begun in Rabat on 13 September and that Erfoud was among the selected settings. Accrocar confirms, on its website and in material supplied to Le Vrai Maroc, a vehicle operation in Rabat in September 2026 involving a Rolls-Royce Cullinan and a Bentley Bentayga.

These layers stay separate. An official renewal is not a location map. A service photograph does not prove an on-screen appearance. Accrocar names two counterparts: [Dune Films](https://dunefilms.com/) and MOONAGE PICTURES (THE GENTLEMEN) LIMITED, UK company 14115797 at Companies House. Accrocar states it has no direct contract with Netflix.

## What remains unconfirmed

No public source reviewed gives a complete Moroccan schedule, precise shooting addresses, scene descriptions or a final vehicle list. Netflix has not publicly confirmed Accrocar as a partner.

Published images are privacy-safe derivatives: metadata was stripped and crops exclude visible plates, people and sensitive site identifiers. Originals remain outside the public website tree.
The Gentlemen is a useful current case, but Morocco's appeal rests on accumulated infrastructure rather than one series.


## How we verified this information

We compared three evidence sets: Netflix announcements, public institutional or media sources, and first-party material supplied by Accrocar. That method explains why “High visual diversity within one territory.” and “Decades of accumulated experience with foreign productions.” may carry different confirmation levels. A date published by Netflix is official; a location named by one outlet remains attributed; an original photograph establishes what is inside the frame, not what happened beyond it.

Every image was checked for vehicle identity, context and sensitive details. We excluded files that revealed a hotel, operational site, registration plate or identifiable person. We did not use an unverified social caption or visual guess to manufacture an address. This restraint makes the story less sensational, but more durable and easier to update when new official information appears.

## Why this matters for Morocco

The Moroccan Cinema Centre’s 2025 report, as covered by the Moroccan business press, puts foreign-production spend in Morocco at MAD 1.213 billion, with MAP citing 48 foreign productions. Television series accounted for MAD 705.55 million. The CCM also describes a cash rebate of up to 30% of certain eligible local costs, subject to conditions. Those figures describe 2025; they do not measure The Gentlemen.

The case also exposes a chain of skills: scouting, line production, unit management, technical crews, accommodation, security, transport and specialised suppliers. An international series does not relocate for one panorama; it assesses whether a territory can repeat a reliable working day.

Rabat and Erfoud test that capacity in different ways. The capital concentrates institutions and services in a varied urban setting; the south-east demands autonomy and control of distance. The professional and economic significance lies in continuity between places, crews and Moroccan businesses, not in an unverifiable promotional claim.
