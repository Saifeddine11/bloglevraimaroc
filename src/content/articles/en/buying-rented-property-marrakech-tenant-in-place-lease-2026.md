A flat sold as "let, immediate income" appeals to a remote investor: no vacancy, no tenant search. The catch is rarely spelled out in the listing. When you buy, you do not get a vacant property, you take over a contract. This article complements our guide to [long-term letting and Law 67-12](/en/articles/long-term-rental-marrakech-law-67-12), this time **from the buyer's side**.

## The lease follows the property: you replace the seller

According to ReaConsult's analysis (14 June 2026), the buyer steps into the seller's rights and obligations under the lease. It continues on the same terms, with the same tenant, until it ends. The reasoning is legal: a lease is a personal right, whereas a sale transfers a real right, ownership.

In practice you collect future rent, but you also take on the landlord's duties:

- quiet enjoyment of the home for the tenant;
- upkeep and repairs that fall on the owner;
- return of the security deposit at the end of the lease, capped at two months' rent excluding charges by article 20 of Law 67-12 (source: Aqarrati).

> "There is no automatic notice to quit because of a sale." — ReaConsult

## The land title does not tell the whole story

The usual reflex is to check the land title. It remains essential, but it shows neither leases, nor rents, nor arrears, nor ongoing disputes. To see what a title is worth, read our article on [land title versus moulkiya](/en/articles/land-title-moulkiya-marrakech).

A ruling of the Casablanca Commercial Court of Appeal of 3 November 2022 (ref. 64648, commented by ReaConsult) illustrates the risk. Co-owners wanted to evict a commercial tenant, arguing that its lease right was not registered on the land title. The Court held that a personal right such as a lease does not need registration to take effect: buyers of a let building take over the obligations of existing leases, even unregistered ones.

A caution: this ruling concerns a commercial lease and is cited only as an illustration. A buyer of a residential flat should not assume that a lease "absent from the title" cannot be enforced against them.

## Freeing the property: a sale is not enough

If you planned to live in the property or resell it vacant, the framework of Law 67-12 (enacted on 19 November 2013, in force since 28 February 2014 according to Aqarrati) is strict:

- the landlord can give notice only when the lease or its renewal expires, and with a legitimate ground;
- accepted grounds include reclaiming the home to live in (the owner, spouse, parent or child) and reconstruction or works that make staying impossible;
- article 45 requires a serious and legitimate reason. If the owner does not actually occupy the property within six months, the tenant can claim damages;
- notice must be served by adoul (notarial-type religious scribe) deed, notarial deed or bailiff's writ: a text message or plain letter is not enough, and defective notice can cause the lease to renew;
- no eviction without a court decision: changing the lock or cutting the water is unlawful.

Sources differ on the minimum term of a residential lease (three years in the tenant's favour for ReaConsult, freely agreed for Aqarrati): have a notary confirm it. For commercial premises, Law 49-16 is described as particularly protective of the tenant, with a possible eviction indemnity that is "often substantial"; we found no reliable scale to quote.

## What this changes for the price

According to ReaConsult, "a let property is not worth a vacant one". Four elements set the gap: the rent in place against the market (above- or below-market rent), the remaining term of the lease, the tenant's quality and the type of lease. A rent below market for several years justifies a discount; steady rent from a stable tenant may deserve a premium. No reliable public figure sets this discount: it is negotiated case by case, hence the value of checking the yield with our method for [net yield after charges and tax](/en/articles/net-rental-yield-marrakech-tax-calculation-2026).

Another point to write into the promise: whether any pre-emption right exists. ReaConsult says it depends on the lease regime and clauses, and recommends a check by a notary or lawyer before listing. See also our article on the [sale agreement versus promise to sell and conditions precedent](/en/articles/sale-agreement-vs-promise-to-sell-marrakech-deposit-conditions-2026).

## Checklist before buying an occupied property

- Ask for the list of occupants and a rent roll (rent, move-in date, expiry).
- Obtain the written lease of each occupant and, for a business, the assignment deeds.
- Ask for recent rent receipts (ReaConsult recommends 24 months) and proof of the security deposit.
- Visit the property to see who actually occupies it.
- Check there is no ongoing dispute with the tenant or the syndic.
- Have a notary confirm the term, renewal and notice conditions that apply.
- Have the security deposit transferred, or deducted from the price in the deed.
- Read the [full purchase procedure](/en/articles/property-buying-process-morocco-step-by-step) before signing.
