Ceuta: verified facts and the positions of Morocco, Spain and the EU

The migration crisis that began in Ceuta, known in Morocco as Sebta, on 30 July 2026 remains a diplomatic issue in early September. Addressing Spain’s parliament on 3 September, Pedro Sánchez said he had no solid evidence establishing that Morocco planned or carried out the episode. Reported accusations must be distinguished from that official position and from investigative findings.

Ceuta is a Spanish-administered city bordering Morocco. Using either its Moroccan or Spanish name does not alter the need to verify individual claims.

What Madrid says

La Moncloa’s 3 September account attributes to Sánchez approximately 70,000 arrivals during the initial episode and around 63,000 returns to Morocco within the first 72 hours. These are figures presented by Spain’s government, not an independent new count made on 5 September.

Sánchez described multiple causes, said investigations would continue and ordered available reports to be published. His statement that there was no solid evidence of a Moroccan operation does not settle every circumstance of the events.

Spain’s EUR 309 million package concerns the local economy, public services, security and reception capacity. It should not be described as an EU grant already disbursed.

European statements and Moroccan cooperation

On 18 August, the European Commission welcomed the results of border cooperation over the preceding weekend and Spain’s announced expansion of reception capacity. It also acknowledged Moroccan statements expressing readiness to take back people who had arrived irregularly, including unaccompanied minors. This is Moroccan positioning reported by an official European source, rather than a directly reproduced Moroccan communiqué.

EFE reported on 3 September that Brussels continued to describe Morocco as a strategic migration partner. Reports on 4 September described progress on emergency assistance; they do not establish an amount actually paid.

Allegations and unresolved questions

On 2 September, Euronews reported accusations that Moroccan agents had directed migrants towards the border. The allegation remains attributed and conditional. A report cited by a media outlet, a political statement and verified evidence are different categories.

Among documents authenticated for this article, we did not identify a dated Moroccan communiqué specifically answering that report. This does not establish that Morocco never responded. Any direct response should be added with its date and original source.

Residents and travel

Reception capacity, local services and the protection of vulnerable people are also central. Demonstrations and political speeches form part of public debate; slogans cannot establish the cause of events or the current number of people present.

The Commission’s official register lists temporary Italian controls on air and sea borders with Spain from 1–16 September. Corresponding Spanish controls with Italy are notified until 7 September. These limited measures do not amount to a general closure of Schengen.

Checking images and figures

Before sharing a video, establish its date, location and earliest publication. Images from 2021 do not automatically document July 2026. Photographs used here show the geographical and border context; they do not claim to depict the current episode or identify vulnerable people.

Common questions

Has a Moroccan-organised operation been established?

Not in the sources verified here. On 3 September, Sánchez said he had no solid evidence to that effect and that investigations continued.

Is the EUR 309 million European aid?

That amount concerns the package announced by Spain’s government. The EU emergency-aid process is separate.

Do the figures describe today’s situation?

The arrival and return figures concern the initial episode as presented by Madrid on 3 September, not a count on 5 September.
