What is a dahir in Morocco? Definition and examples

The word dahir appears in Moroccan legal texts, land references and some real-estate documents. Here is a plain-language definition for non-lawyers.

R
Le Vrai Maroc editorial team· Editorial analysis
July 17, 202614min readUpdated on July 17, 2026
Simple Definition

A dahir is a royal act used in the Moroccan legal order. Depending on context, it may promulgate a law, organize certain decisions or appear in official references that structure Moroccan legal life.

This article provides general information and does not replace advice from a Moroccan notary, lawyer or legal professional.

Simple definition and legal role

In everyday terms, a dahir is a royal act. Many Moroccan texts use the formula “Dahir promulgating Law…” This means that an adopted law enters the legal order through promulgation and publication.

The exact role depends on context. For a non-lawyer, the key point is that a dahir is not a brochure, private contract or commercial note. It belongs to the vocabulary of official acts.

Dahir, law, decree and order

A law sets rules in legislative matters. A dahir can promulgate that law. A decree is a regulatory act that often applies or organizes rules. An order is generally a more targeted administrative act. In a real-estate file, several of these references may appear, and the buyer should ask a professional which text actually applies.

DahirRoyal act, often linked to promulgation or specific powers.
LawText adopted in legislative areas.
DecreeRegulatory act of application or organization.
OrderMore targeted administrative act.

Publication in the Official Bulletin

The Official Bulletin is the reference source for checking publication of a text. To cite a text, identify its title, date, subject, publication and later amendments.

What is the link between a real-estate dahir and S.A.F.E?

Independent editorial methodology — not a public certification or legal opinion.

A dahir is part of the Moroccan legal framework. The S.A.F.E legal-check grid is not a law, not an official certification and not legal advice. It simply helps organize the questions a buyer should have verified in a real-estate file.

The legal pillar of S.A.F.E identifies applicable Moroccan texts. Depending on the transaction, those references may include dahirs, laws, decrees, orders, land-registration rules, urban-planning provisions and contractual obligations. A buyer should not merely see the word “dahir” and feel reassured; the exact reference, date, scope, version and practical relevance must be verified by a qualified professional.

The dahir in real-estate verification

In real-estate verification, a dahir may appear indirectly in land registration, ownership, real rights, registration procedures, publication and enforceability. The useful question is practical: what does this reference change for the title, contract, permit, certificate or sale?

This reading joins the logic of S.A.F.E real-estate verification: organize checks before committing, then have legal consequences confirmed by a professional. For investment context, see investing in Marrakech in 2026.

How to verify a legal reference

Identify the exact title, date, subject, law if promulgated, Official Bulletin number and amendments. Then ask a notary, lawyer or competent professional to confirm scope and relevance.

FAQ

What is a dahir in Morocco?

A dahir is a royal act used in the Moroccan legal order. It can notably promulgate a law or intervene in certain areas provided by the institutional framework.

Is a dahir the same as a law?

Not exactly. A law is adopted in legislative matters; a dahir may promulgate a law. Meaning depends on the text concerned.

Why does a dahir matter in real estate?

Several Moroccan land texts, especially around land registration, are referenced by dahir. Buyers encounter them through title, certificates and deeds.

Where can a dahir be checked?

The reference source is the Official Bulletin through the General Secretariat of Government. In a transaction, verification should be confirmed by a notary or lawyer.
